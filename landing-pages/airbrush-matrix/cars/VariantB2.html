The Process

A Body Shop Would Respect This Paint Job

Getting a supercar livery onto a sneaker isn't a print job. It's days of brushwork on a curved leather canvas. Here's exactly how a KNICKGASM car custom gets built.

01

Start with the real thing

Every custom begins as a brand-new, 100% original Nike Air Force 1. No replicas, no blanks, no shortcuts. The base pair is the same one you'd buy at retail — that's the point.

The leather is prepped and cleaned so paint bonds to the panel, not just sits on it.

02

Sketch the livery

A car livery lives on flat body panels. A sneaker is all curves — toe box, swoosh, quarter panel, heel. Our artists redraw the design to flow with the shoe: where the racing stripe breaks, where the badge sits, how the sponsor marks wrap the mudguard.

Colour matching is obsessive. Rosso corsa is not "red". Papaya is not "orange". Bayside blue is not "blue".

03

Hand-paint, layer by layer

Base coats first, then panel colour, then detail work — logos, numbers, pinstripes — built up over multiple sessions by India's best sneaker artists. Because it's brushwork, no two pairs are ever identical. Yours is a one-of-one by definition.

04

Seal it for the street

The finished pair gets a protective seal, making the design water and scratch resistant. These customs are built to be in your rotation, not behind glass. The whole build is made to order — allow 10–15 days from order to doorstep, with express shipping to 60+ countries.

Copywriting & Business Frameworks

The swipe library every generator writes from. Following the retention teams we studied (Chase Dimond's first rule: never write from a blank page, always start from a proven framework), every mailer and campaign brief now opens by picking the best-fit structure below and building the copy to its beats. This page is the human-readable mirror of api/_shared/copy-frameworks.js, which the Mailer Calendar, Plan Calendar and Mailer Studio all call.

How it works. When a mailer is generated, the system chooses one framework (by the play or content type, deterministically, so the same slot always picks the same one), then instructs the model to map that framework onto the email: the opening beat becomes the hero, the middle beats become the body sections in order, and the final beat lands on the button. The chosen framework is recorded on the output so you can see it in the preview. You can override it per slot with a framework field.

Copywriting frameworks (how one email is structured)

PASProblem, Agitate, Solution
  1. ProblemName the small friction the reader already feels, in their words, not the brand's.
  2. AgitateMake the cost of it vivid and human, never fear-based. No medical or anxiety claims.
  3. SolutionPresent the product as the calm resolution, with one clear next step.
the problem they already have, what it costs them each week, the product as the plain fix.
Best for: win-back, problem-aware audiences, benefit-led stories.
BABBefore, After, Bridge
  1. BeforeTheir world today, honest and familiar, no judgement.
  2. AfterThe better ordinary once the routine lands, sensory and believable.
  3. BridgeThe product as the short path from one to the other, one CTA.
how their week looks today, how it looks once the product is part of it, the one step between.
Best for: brand intros, cross-sell to an adjacent product, subscription framing.
AIDAAttention, Interest, Desire, Action
  1. AttentionA sensory hook in the first line, a scent, a sound, a moment.
  2. InterestWhy it is relevant now, the launch, the season.
  3. DesireMake it wanted with proof the brand's own record holds: its claims, its stated prices.
  4. ActionOne clear CTA, no competing links.
something new in the range, why it matters now, the proof on record, one link to see it.
Best for: launch announcements and offer-forward closers.
4PsPromise, Picture, Proof, Push
  1. PromiseThe outcome the reader gets, stated plainly.
  2. PicturePaint it: the moment in the reader's day where it fits.
  3. ProofThe value math the brand can stand behind: its own prices and approved claims.
  4. PushThe ask, framed by the play's CTA rule.
what they get, what it looks like in their week, the price and proof from the brand's record, the ask.
Best for: subscription value-math, B2G1, any proof-led offer.
FABFeature, Advantage, Benefit
  1. FeatureWhat it is, the concrete thing in the box.
  2. AdvantageWhat that does differently.
  3. BenefitWhat it means for the reader's day.
what is in the box, what it does that the alternative does not, what that means for their day.
Best for: education and unboxing sends where showing beats telling.
Star-Story-Solution
  1. StarThe hero: the product, a routine, the moment it fits into. The brand speaks as "we", never a founder or personal narrator.
  2. StoryIts origin and journey, where it comes from, how it is made.
  3. SolutionInvite the reader into that story with one warm CTA.
where the product comes from, how it is made, the one place to start.
Best for: origin and brand-story sends, editorial tone, trust before offer.

Business frameworks (what to send and how hard to push)

The strategy layer. These set the offer posture behind a campaign, not the sentence structure. Each cohort's brief carries the ones that apply.

Hammer vs Nurture  Jimmy Kim
Match send intensity to intent. Engaged, high-intent contacts can take a direct offer; cold or lapsed contacts need value and story first. Mismatching burns deliverability and trust. Both current cohorts are non-engagers, so both lean nurture.
RFM whale / minnow priority  Drew Sanocki
Not every contact is worth the same touch. Prioritize by Recency, Frequency, Monetary value; spend the richest creative where lifetime value is highest, keep cold-list touches low-cost and low-pressure.
Three Multipliers  Drew Sanocki
Revenue = buyers x average order value x purchase frequency. Every campaign should move at least one lever deliberately: acquire a first order, lift AOV (bundles, Pack of 3), or raise frequency (subscription).
Discount ladder  Resource library
Earn the sale with value before price. Climb in order: content and story, then education and proof, then existing mechanics (subscription pricing, B2G1, the 7 gifts). Only then any price lever. Never invent new codes.
Brand-first performance  Cody Plofker
Lead with brand codes, sourcing and education rather than discount hooks. It produces customers with better retention curves, not just cheaper clicks.
Merchandising is retention  Ari Murray
The reason a lapsed customer re-buys is usually the offer and its presentation, not the automation that delivered it. Treat the bundle, the gift stack and the page as retention levers.

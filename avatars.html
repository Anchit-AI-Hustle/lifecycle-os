How avatars fit the rest of the OS. Cohorts (in the Cohorts feature) are the raw lenses: RFM segments, engagement cohorts, lifecycle stages, product cohorts. An Avatar is a curated composite of those lenses plus market truth, given a name and a face. Analytics assigns a live customer to cohorts; the avatar tells the creative team what that person wants, fears and responds to. The market study underneath these avatars lives at docs/market-intelligence/us-coffee-d2c-landscape.md (a legacy filename kept because several pages link to that exact path; the contents are the custom-sneaker market study); the live profile fields are captured by schemas/cohort-profile.json; the automated actions are wired in config/retention-triggers.yaml.

The Four Avatars V2 - active

Four behavioural buyer profiles. Each maps to specific cohorts, drawn verbatim from the platform Cohorts dictionary (Engagement Cohorts A to F, Lifecycle Stages, RFM Segments, Product and Behavioral cohorts), so a brief can be written against a person rather than a percentage. The behaviour on each card is derived from those cohort definitions. Demographic and geographic profile is not stated: this deployment holds no sourced audience research for it, and an invented age band or income range would read as measured. Each of those fields carries its own data marker until a named, dated source is wired in.

One customer can resolve to different avatars over their lifetime as their cohorts change. Always target by the avatar a campaign is written for, and keep the name verbatim across mailer, ad, landing-page and social briefs. Source: the Cohorts dictionary in this deployment. Every behavioural line above restates a cohort definition; nothing on this page is an estimate of audience size, income or geography, and the fields that would need one say so.

## 3. Verified brand work and documented results

**Karmaloop (the canonical retention turnaround).** Karmaloop was an iconic streetwear e-tailer that had grown to ~$100M in revenue, then collapsed into bankruptcy after pouring VC money into every acquisition channel while ignoring retention and training customers to only buy on discount. Sanocki came in as CMO after the acquisition out of bankruptcy. His documented diagnosis and fix (published in detail via Appcues/Price Intelligently and his own writing):

- Root cause: "too much emphasis on acquisition, not enough on retention" — a leaky bucket refilled with ever-cheaper, discount-driven customers.
- Fix: a full lifecycle marketing system — cart abandonment, post-purchase, win-back, and "trap wire" trigger emails at key lifecycle moments — plus a shift from blanket discounting to monetizing loyal, willing-to-pay customers (ARPU focus).
- Documented outcome: the trap-wire lifecycle campaigns delivered ~500% ROI and were among the company's most effective programs; the turnaround narrative ("bankruptcy back to eight figures in under a year") is repeated across multiple independent publications. The business was subsequently sold.

**AutoAnything.** As CEO, facing Black Friday bidding wars against Amazon/Walmart/AutoZone, his team built a click-to-conversion latency attribution model, front-loaded paid spend into the cheaper discovery weeks before BFCM, then leaned on owned channels — email, SMS, and direct mail — during peak week. This case is told consistently in PostPilot's own content and third-party podcasts.

**PostPilot.** The platform's public case studies name real brands (e.g., a >3.8x ROAS prospecting result for Caden Lane via their "Cardalog" format) and the co-founders openly used Klaviyo since ~2012 at their own brands (Klaviyo's founders personally helped set up Karmaloop's account in their Boston office — a charming, verifiable-by-Klaviyo detail from PostPilot's Premier Partner announcement).

## 4. The Sanocki playbook: distinctive ideas worth internalizing

- **Whale/minnow (RFM) segmentation.** His most famous idea: your top ~10% of customers ("whales," identified via Recency-Frequency-Monetary analysis) drive 40%+ of revenue; the bottom "minnows" cost you money. Identify whales, give them VIP treatment, let low-value discount-chasers churn. Every retention program should start with this sort.
- **The Three Multipliers.** Revenue = Customers × AOV × Purchase Frequency. Most brands only ever pull the first lever (more customers); retention marketing is the discipline of pulling the other two, which are usually cheaper.
- **Fix the leaky bucket before pouring water.** New-customer acquisition costs 5–7x more than retention; acquisition-first thinking on top of poor retention is value destruction. Retention drives LTV, LTV funds acquisition, acquisition feeds retention — one loop, not two departments.
- **Discounting attracts discount customers.** From Karmaloop: when you discount your way to growth, you replace full-margin buyers with deal-seekers and permanently damage price integrity. His BFCM warning: brands often lose money in Cyber Week by swapping full-margin purchases for discounted ones — model it before celebrating topline.
- **Trap wires / trigger marketing.** Automated interventions at behaviorally defined lifecycle moments (about-to-churn windows derived from historical inter-purchase latency), rather than calendar-based blasts.
- **Direct mail is the forgotten retention channel.** 20–50% of a brand's customer file has no good email address, and inbox open rates cap what email can reach. Postcards for win-backs, abandoned carts (MailMatch — mailing customers for whom you only have an email), and retargeting anonymous site visitors (SiteMatch) extend lifecycle marketing offline with email-like automation. He is openly biased here (it's his company), but the underlying reachability math is sound and checkable.
- **Profit metrics over growth metrics.** CLV, payback period, contribution margin — "the numbers that determine if you're building a business or a bonfire."

## 8. Field checklist: running the Sanocki retention audit on any brand

His turnaround method, compressed into a repeatable diagnostic you can run in a week with order data alone:

1. **Export 24 months of orders and run the RFM sort.** Score every customer on recency, frequency, and monetary value; define whales as the top decile by value. Compute what share of revenue they carry — if it's ~40%+ (typical), your marketing budget allocation should look nothing like it currently does.
2. **Baseline the Three Multipliers.** Customers × AOV × Frequency for the last 12 months. Then model a 10% lift in each independently — the exercise almost always shows AOV/frequency lifts are cheaper than equivalent acquisition, which is the argument for retention budget.
3. **Find the churn window from purchase latency.** Plot the distribution of days-between-orders; the point where most repeaters have already reordered (e.g., day 60–90) is where your "trap wire" win-back must fire — with escalating offers only to non-responders (his discount ladder: start soft/no discount, escalate 10→15→20% only as silence persists, never opening with the max).
4. **Segment the offer policy.** Whales get access, service, and early drops — never blanket discounts. Discount-acquired one-timers get the ladder. This single policy change is the core of the Karmaloop fix.
5. **Instrument profit metrics next to growth metrics:** CLV by cohort, CAC payback period, contribution margin per order. Kill or cap any acquisition channel whose payback exceeds your cash tolerance.
6. **Test one offline touch with a holdout.** Pull your unreachable file (bounced/never-opened buyers) and lapsed whales; send a postcard win-back against a randomized holdout and measure incremental reorder rate. Whether or not you use PostPilot, the holdout discipline is the point.
7. **Pre-mortem your BFCM.** Model how much peak-week revenue is margin-swapped demand (customers who'd have bought at full price) versus incremental — his Karmaloop lesson that a record topline can hide a loss.

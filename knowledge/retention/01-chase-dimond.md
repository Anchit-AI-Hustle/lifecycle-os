## 4. What he actually teaches: the Dimond playbook

His content clusters into five recurring themes, and knowing these lets you navigate his archive efficiently:

**Copywriting by framework, never from scratch.** One of his most-shared theses: his agency has written 10,000+ pieces of copy and their #1 rule is to always start from a proven framework (AIDA, PAS, BAB, 4Ps, etc.). He has published threads breaking down 10+ copywriting frameworks specifically adapted to ecommerce email. The practical implication for a retention team: build a swipe/framework library before you build a content calendar.

**Flows before campaigns, then campaigns feed flows.** He treats automated flows (welcome, abandoned cart/browse, post-purchase, win-back) as the compounding revenue base — the "press send and know what happens" predictability layer — and campaigns as the demand-creation layer that pushes people back into flow-triggering behaviors. He argued as early as the iOS 14 era that email's value is owning a predictable, audience-controlled channel as paid signal degrades.

**The 30% benchmark.** A recurring performance anchor in his interviews: healthy ecommerce brands should see roughly 30% of total revenue attributable to email (his brands "get 30% of their sales from email"). This is a useful diagnostic number to benchmark any DTC brand's retention program against.

**Campaign calendar density.** His Master Campaign Calendar product (96 campaign ideas across a year) reflects a core belief: most brands under-send and under-plan. He pushes brands toward a always-on calendar of reasons to email beyond discounts — content, education, launches, community, and seasonal hooks.

**List growth as the upstream constraint.** Pop-up strategy, opt-in offers, and subscriber quality get heavy coverage; he has published guides like "0 to 500K email subscribers in 10 months" and Klaviyo subscriber-enrichment guides.

Use Chase for breadth: campaign ideas, copy frameworks, flow architectures, benchmarks, and "what the top 1% of brands are sending." ## 8. Field checklist: installing the Dimond system in your own brand

This converts his teachings into an executable sequence. None of it requires his paid products.

1. **Build the framework library first.** Before writing a single email, document 5–10 copywriting frameworks (PAS, AIDA, BAB, 4Ps, FAB, star-story-solution) with one filled-in ecommerce example each. Every future brief starts by picking a framework, per his agency's #1 rule.
2. **Stand up the core flow stack in priority order:** welcome (buyer and non-buyer variants), abandoned checkout, abandoned browse, post-purchase (first-time vs. repeat), win-back, and sunset. His published welcome-series-for-non-buyers guide is the free blueprint for the highest-leverage one.
3. **Run the 30% audit.** Pull last 90 days of revenue and compute email-attributed share. Under ~20%: fix flows and list capture before touching campaigns. At 25–35%: shift energy to campaign creativity and segmentation depth. Materially above 40%: check attribution settings before celebrating — over-attribution is common.
4. **Densify the campaign calendar.** Map 8–12 sends per month across four buckets — promotional, educational, community/brand, and product/launch — so discounts are never more than ~a third of volume. His 96-ideas calendar exists precisely because "nothing to send" is a planning failure, not a content failure.
5. **Institutionalize swipe research.** A 20-minute weekly session in Inboox.ai or your own competitor-subscription inbox, filed into a team swipe folder tagged by flow type, keeps creative from going stale.
6. **Grow the list upstream.** Test pop-up timing, offer, and two-step designs quarterly; capture SMS alongside email; enrich profiles progressively (his Klaviyo enrichment guide covers the mechanics).
7. **Study the Friday critiques.** His "Alex in My Inbox" series is effectively a free weekly copy-review apprenticeship — read five back-issues before writing your next campaign.

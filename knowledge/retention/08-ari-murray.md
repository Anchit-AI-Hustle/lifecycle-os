## 1. Who she is and why she matters

Ari Murray covers the part of retention nobody else in this pool does: **what the actual emails, offers, and pages should look like**. Frameworks (Sanocki), systems (Kim), data (Perrelli), and CX (Weiss) all still require someone to conceive the campaign, write the subject line, and merchandise the landing page — and Ari's entire public output is that layer, dropped from live client work at Sharma Brands, the New York DTC consultancy founded by Nik Sharma ("the DTC guy").

Her differentiation, in her own words: most marketing content is people on stage talking about work; hers is written "from someone who is literally behind the scenes doing it" — she still works full-time as an executive on brands by day and writes the newsletter at night specifically so the advice stays current and first-hand. That operating constraint is why Go-to-Millions reads like a swipe file with commentary rather than thought leadership.

## 4. The Murray playbook: distinctive ideas worth internalizing

- **Study brands like a funnel hacker.** Her recurring format: pick a brand (e.g., "To Patagonia, with Love"), walk its site, sign up for its emails, screenshot everything, and annotate what's working. She frames buying from competitors and dissecting their post-purchase experience as the highest-ROI research a retention marketer can do.
- **Swipe files as infrastructure.** Signature recurring editions: subject-line copy swipe files, back-in-stock notification breakdowns ("the art of the back-in-stock notification"), BFCM "website north stars" (11 sites that merchandised their sale perfectly), and seasonal product-drop strategies. The meta-lesson: maintain a living library of executions, organized by job-to-be-done.
- **Merchandising IS retention.** Her content treats landing pages, bundles, drops, and offer construction as retention levers — the reason a past customer opens and re-buys is usually the offer and its presentation, not the automation that delivered it.
- **Post-sale details compound.** Small verified example of her thinking: leave discount codes active briefly after a sale ends (Cyber Monday tip) — capturing stragglers costs nothing and prevents the worst post-purchase feeling (missing the deal by hours).
- **Ship, don't polish.** Her stated content philosophy — don't let perfection block output; ideas come from doing the job daily — mirrors her advice to brands on campaign velocity: more good sends beat fewer perfect ones.
- **Voice as a moat.** Go-to-Millions' consistent persona ("Bonjour Millionaire," the 🍀 subject-line mark, integrated text-native sponsorships) is itself a masterclass in the email craft she preaches — the newsletter is the case study.

## 8. Field checklist: installing the Murray creative-research system

Her working method, converted into a repeatable operating rhythm for any retention/growth team:

1. **Run a monthly competitor purchase audit.** Actually buy from two competitors or admired brands; screenshot every touch from pop-up to post-purchase to win-back for 45 days. File the screenshots, annotate what you'd steal, and put one stolen idea into your own calendar each month — her funnel-hacking practice systematized.
2. **Build the swipe file by job-to-be-done, not by brand:** folders for welcome, back-in-stock, launch/drop, replenishment, win-back, BFCM merchandising, and subject lines. A swipe file organized by brand is a museum; organized by job, it's a toolbox.
3. **Master the back-in-stock and drop mechanics.** Her recurring teardown subjects for a reason: restock notifications and product-drop sequences convert warm demand at near-zero cost, and most brands under-design them (no waitlist capture, no urgency logic, no second notice).
4. **Merchandise your sales like the "north star" sites.** Before any major promo, redesign the homepage/collection hierarchy around the offer (dedicated sale collections, updated heroes, bundle logic) — the level of site-side effort her BFCM roundups document at winning brands.
5. **Add the post-sale grace window.** Keep discount codes quietly active for a short period after a sale "ends" to convert stragglers without training lateness — her Cyber Monday tip, generalizable to every promo.
6. **Set a velocity norm.** Adopt her ship-don't-polish rule as a team SLA: a good campaign out this week beats a perfect one in three; retro on results, not on drafts.
7. **Develop a written voice document.** Codify greeting, sign-off, emoji/formatting rules, and sponsorship/cross-sell integration style the way Go-to-Millions does ("Bonjour Millionaire," the 🍀, read-time labels) — consistency is what makes an owned channel feel like a destination.
8. **If you build a newsletter/media asset:** copy her deliverability-and-engagement openers (welcome email that asks a question and requests a reply) and the weekday-matched depth cadence (short tactical mid-week, long-form Sunday).

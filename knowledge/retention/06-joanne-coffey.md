## 4. The Coffey playbook: distinctive ideas worth internalizing

- **Zero-party data is the retention unlock.** Ask customers directly (quizzes, surveys, review prompts), store the answers as profile properties, and let every flow branch on them. Personalization built on declared data beats inferred data — and it compounds: the same quiz powers acquisition conversion, welcome personalization, and replenishment logic.
- **Segment on sentiment and behavior, not just recency.** Review scores, support history, and product owned should change the message. A one-size welcome/win-back is a wasted asset.
- **Plain-text beats designed when the goal is relationship.** Her save-flow emails deliberately look like a human wrote them, because the job of that email is conversation, not conversion.
- **Test loudly, share results.** Her reputation was built on posting actual A/B outcomes (subject lines, send times, flow splits) — the practice of treating every send as an experiment and every result as content.
- **Personal brand as career leverage.** She explicitly attributes the Jones Road opportunity to taking her public presence seriously — a meta-lesson she teaches younger retention marketers.

## 8. Field checklist: building the Coffey zero-party pipeline

Her documented Jones Road system, reduced to an implementation order any Klaviyo/Postscript-class stack can follow:

1. **Design a quiz that solves a real pre-purchase problem** (shade match, formula match, gift finder, dosage/size finder) — not a fluffy personality quiz. The utility is what earns completion; the answers are the asset.
2. **Sync every answer as a profile property** in your ESP, named cleanly (skin_type, concern, product_interest, purchase_frequency), so flows and campaigns can branch on them for years. Her exact phrasing of the value: direct customer inputs become "the bridge" to personalized interactions.
3. **Fork the welcome flow on quiz completion.** Quiz-takers get recommendations referencing their answers; non-takers get the standard series plus a quiz invite. Benchmark against her published result — a 20% higher placed-order rate for the quiz path — to know whether yours is working.
4. **Personalize inside one campaign, not with ten campaigns.** Master conditional (hide/show) blocks so a single send renders differently by product owned, quiz answers, or VIP status; this is the technique her TYCA episode walks through and the reason her segment counts stayed manageable.
5. **Wire reviews into segmentation.** Pipe review ratings into profiles (any reviews-to-ESP integration works) and split at minimum: 5-star advocates (referral/UGC asks), 3–4 star (education/cross-sell), 1–2 star (service recovery, suppress promos). Never send an enthusiastic upsell to a documented unhappy customer.
6. **Build the human-voiced save flow.** At-risk definition from your reorder curve → plain-text email "from" a real senior person asking what went wrong → route replies to CX with authority to fix. Measure saves and reply-derived insights, not clicks.
7. **Keep a public-grade test log.** Her career was built on recording every A/B (hypothesis, variant, result, decision) clearly enough to share — do the same internally even if you never post it; it becomes onboarding material and prevents re-testing old losers.
8. **Treat SMS as a distinct voice, not email's echo.** Her Postscript-credited work was "hyper-personalized" — segment-specific, conversational, quiz-informed texts — rather than campaign mirrors.

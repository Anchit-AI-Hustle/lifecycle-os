## 1. Who she is and why she matters

Gina Perrelli — a self-described "retention girlie" of 12+ years — owns the most under-taught corner of retention marketing: **subscriptions**. Email and SMS retention get the spotlight, but for replenishable and consumable products, the subscription program *is* the retention program, and almost nobody publishes real subscription retention mechanics. Perrelli does, because she built the tooling: first as the head of a retention agency division serving dozens of CPG brands, then as CEO of Stay AI, a subscription platform whose entire pitch is turning subscription management from a payment collector into a performance channel.

Independent DTC-Twitter roundups flagged her early as a must-follow retention specialist (then via Retextion — an earlier name in her subscription-tool lineage — and Lunar Solar Group), noting she shares her CRM team's actual emails and churn-reduction examples rather than platitudes.

## 4. The Perrelli playbook: distinctive ideas worth internalizing

- **Subscription is a performance channel, not a billing feature.** Her founding insight: legacy tools let you collect payments but not *market* to subscribers — no A/B tests, no offers, no experimentation. "Once you got a subscriber, you just hoped they didn't cancel." Everything she teaches flows from inverting that.
- **Know your churn curve before designing anything.** Her canonical example (shared publicly via Staylien): a brand wanted a punch-card reward at orders 6 and 12; the data showed **half of subscribers churn by order 2, only 13.5% reach order 5, and 1.6% reach order 10** — so the rewards would have gone exclusively to customers who were staying anyway. Retention interventions must target the cliff (orders 1–5), not the plateau.
- **A/B test the retention offer itself.** Free gift on order 2 vs. none, with a control group and clean postage/fulfillment logic — the experiment legacy tools literally couldn't run. Testing gifts, cross-sells, and swap offers against holdouts is the core Stay methodology (the "ExperienceEngine").
- **Active + passive churn are different problems.** Cancel flows (active churn: intercept, diagnose reason, counter-offer) and dunning (passive churn: failed payments, card expiry) need separate systems; AI's near-term wins are in predicting who's about to churn and timing the intervention before the cancel click.
- **Cancel reasons are strategy input.** Cancel-flow surveys are the cheapest qualitative research a consumables brand can run — reasons feed product, shipping-cadence, and pricing decisions, not just save offers.
- **Remove opinion from email/SMS.** Her agency-era doctrine: structure accounts for testing so decisions come from controlled experiments, not taste — a discipline she institutionalized with a dedicated analyst pod.

Use Gina for anything subscription-shaped: designing cancel flows, sequencing win-back offers, deciding where loyalty rewards belong on the churn curve, structuring gift/cross-sell experiments with holdouts, and dunning strategy. ## 8. Field checklist: running the Perrelli subscription audit

Her method, expressed as a sequence any subscription (or repeat-purchase) brand can execute regardless of platform:

1. **Plot the survival curve first.** Percentage of subscribers reaching order 1, 2, 3… 10. Her reference pattern — ~50% gone by order 2, ~13.5% reaching order 5, ~1.6% reaching order 10 — is a calibration line: if your curve looks like that, orders 1–4 are the battlefield and everything else is decoration.
2. **Place interventions at the cliff, not the plateau.** Free gifts, punch-card rewards, and loyalty milestones belong where churn concentrates (typically orders 2–4), never at aspirational order counts most subscribers will never reach — the exact mistake her Staylien example caught.
3. **Rebuild the cancel flow as a diagnostic + save engine:** capture the reason (required, structured), then branch the save offer to the reason — "too much product" → skip/pause/frequency change; "too expensive" → downsize or discount ladder; "didn't work" → education/CX handoff. A generic "20% off to stay" for every reason wastes margin on the wrong people.
4. **Run every retention offer against a holdout.** Gift-on-order-2 vs. control; swap-offer vs. control; win-back discount vs. control. If your tooling can't hold out a control group, that's a tooling problem — the gap that motivated Stay's ExperienceEngine — not a reason to skip measurement.
5. **Separate passive churn ops.** Dunning (retry logic, card-updater, pre-expiry emails/SMS) is its own workstream with its own dashboard; brands routinely lose more to failed payments than to cancels and never notice because both hide inside "churn."
6. **Mine cancel reasons monthly as strategy input.** Route the structured reasons to product (formulation/size), ops (shipping cadence defaults), and pricing — her framing of cancel surveys as the cheapest qualitative research a consumables brand owns.
7. **De-opinionate the program.** Her agency-era structure — accounts organized around a standing test roadmap (cadence, send-time, creative, offer) with an analyst reviewing controls — is replicable at small scale with one spreadsheet and discipline.
8. **Translate for non-subscription brands:** the same audit works on reorder curves — replace "order number" with "purchase number" and place win-backs at the latency cliff.

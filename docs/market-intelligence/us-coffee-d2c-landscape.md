## 1. Macro Unit Economics & Sizing (2026/2027)

### Market sizing (top-down, US only)

| Layer | Definition | Est. 2026/2027 Value | Key Assumptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | Total US retail coffee (in-home + away-from-home, all channels) | **~$28B/yr** retail; ~$45B incl. foodservice | US coffee retail growing ~4-5% CAGR; ~66% of US adults (≈160M) drink coffee daily, avg spend ~$175/yr in-home. |
| **SAM** | US **coffee D2C + specialty/premium** reachable online (subscription + one-time ecommerce, whole bean/pods/instant/functional) | **~$4.2B/yr** (≈15% of retail flowing through D2C/online-specialty) | D2C + online specialty is ~12-16% of retail and taking ~1.5-2 pts/yr from grocery; excludes pure-grocery private label. |
| **SOM (KNICKGASM 3-yr)** | Realistic share a premium heritage brand can capture in **functional/streetwear coffee** (Airbrush coffee, adaptogen colorways, supplements-adjacent) | **~$18M-$40M/yr** achievable in 24-36 mo | Functional coffee is ~$900M-$1.1B of the SAM and growing ~9-12% CAGR; a focused brand capturing 2-4% of that niche = $18-40M. |

**Sizing logic.** The whole US coffee retail pie (~$28B) is the TAM but most of it is commoditized grocery. The winnable slice is the D2C + online-specialty SAM (~$4.2B), and within that the fastest-growing, least price-anchored pocket is **functional/streetwear coffee** (~$1B and compounding double-digit). KNICKGASM's heritage-sneaker credibility + adaptogen sourcing story lets it play there rather than fighting Folgers on shelf price. A 2-4% niche capture over 3 years is aggressive but defensible given an existing customer base and owned lifecycle tooling.

### Blended industry benchmarks

| Metric | Standard Coffee D2C | Functional / Streetwear Coffee |
|---|---|---|
| Blended CAC (paid + organic) | **$28-$42** | **$45-$70** |
| Paid-only CAC (Meta/TikTok) | $38-$60 | $65-$95 |
| Day-0 conversion (first order, session→purchase) | 2.4-3.2% | 1.8-2.6% |
| Day-30 retention (repeat or active sub) | 32-40% | 42-55% |
| Day-180 retention | 18-24% | 30-42% |
| Average Order Value (AOV) | $34-$48 | $52-$78 |
| Subscription attach rate (at checkout) | 22-30% | 35-48% |
| 12-mo LTV (blended) | $95-$140 | $180-$290 |
| LTV:CAC ratio | 2.8:1 - 3.6:1 | 3.4:1 - 4.6:1 |
| Blended gross margin | 55-64% | 62-72% |
| Payback period (months) | 3.5-5.5 | 4.0-6.5 |

**Read.** Functional coffee carries a materially higher CAC (education-heavy, ingredient-skeptic buyer) but pays it back with **higher AOV, dramatically higher subscription attach, and 2x the Day-180 retention**. That is the entire strategic case: acquire at a premium, retain like a supplement, monetize like a subscription. KNICKGASM's Lifecycle OS should be tuned to defend the Day-30→Day-180 retention cliff, where functional brands win or die.

## 3. Growth Cohorts & Behavioral Segments

### Cohort A — "The Optimizer" (Functionality-driven)
- **Demographics.** Age 28-42, 58% male / 42% female, HHI $90K-$160K. Skews knowledge-worker, fitness/biohacking-adjacent.
- **Geography.** High-index: California, Washington, Colorado, Texas (Austin), Massachusetts. Zip archetype: **Urban Coastal** + affluent tech-suburb.
- **Mechanics.** Price elasticity **low-moderate** (will pay premium for proof, not for hype). Core value driver: **Functionality** (grail-drop/stress, focus, clean energy). Reactivation likelihood **high** if re-engaged with new-benefit or lab-backed content. Churn triggers: "didn't feel a difference" at ~week 3, competitor with louder efficacy claim.

### Cohort B — "The Ritualist" (Routine-driven)
- **Demographics.** Age 35-55, 63% female / 37% male, HHI $75K-$130K. Streetwear-lifestyle, yoga/meditation, existing sneaker drinkers.
- **Geography.** High-index: New York, New Jersey, Illinois, Oregon, North Carolina. Zip archetype: **Affluent Suburban** + inner-ring urban.
- **Mechanics.** Price elasticity **low** (routine = habit = sticky). Core value driver: **Routine** (morning ritual, calm energy, replaces afternoon crash). Reactivation likelihood **very high** (routine re-forms easily with a replenishment nudge). Churn triggers: cadence mismatch (too much/little coffee shipped), life disruption (travel/move).

### Cohort C — "The Status Seeker" (Status-driven)
- **Demographics.** Age 30-50, 51% female / 49% male, HHI $140K-$300K+. Design-conscious, gifting-heavy, premium-brand collectors.
- **Geography.** High-index: California (LA/SF), New York (Manhattan/Brooklyn), Florida (Miami), Texas (Dallas). Zip archetype: **Urban Coastal luxury** + high-net-worth suburban enclave.
- **Mechanics.** Price elasticity **very low** (price is a signal, not a barrier). Core value driver: **Status** (heritage, one-of-one provenance, limited drops, beautiful packaging). Reactivation likelihood **moderate** (needs a novelty/exclusive hook, not a discount). Churn triggers: brand feels "mass," discount-heavy messaging that cheapens the halo.

### Cohort D — "The Curious Convert" (Trial-driven, streetwear-curious)
- **Demographics.** Age 24-38, 60% female / 40% male, HHI $55K-$95K. First-time functional-coffee buyer, price-aware, review-driven.
- **Geography.** Broad but indexes in Georgia, Arizona, Tennessee, Ohio, Pennsylvania. Zip archetype: **Affluent Suburban** + emerging urban.
- **Mechanics.** Price elasticity **high** (converts on intro offer/starter kit). Core value driver: mix of **Functionality + Routine**, gated by **trust**. Reactivation likelihood **moderate-high** if second-order nudge lands with social proof. Churn triggers: first-bag price shock after intro discount, unmet expectation, choice overload.

**Cohort-to-product mapping.** Optimizer → Coffee Collection + focus/adaptogen SKUs on subscription. Ritualist → coffee + sneaker multi-format routine bundle. Status Seeker → one-of-one limited drops + premium gift sets. Curious Convert → starter kit / sampler → second-order nudge → subscribe-and-save.

These figures are **blended analyst-style estimates assembled for internal planning**, not audited or company-reported numbers.

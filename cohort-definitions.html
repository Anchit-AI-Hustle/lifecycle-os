How the lenses fit together. Every customer sits in one Lifecycle Stage (where they are in the journey), one RFM Segment (how valuable and recent), one Engagement Level (how they treat our email), and one or more Product Cohorts (what they buy). The Engagement Cohorts are purchase history crossed with engagement level; Cohorts A and B are the specific dormant slices we work right now. Behavioral cohorts cut across everything by interest, so creative can be themed. Pick the lens the task needs: targeting starts with Engagement or Product, cadence uses RFM, journey logic uses Lifecycle Stage, and theming uses Behavioral.

Engagement Cohorts V2 - active program

Purchase history crossed with email engagement, the full matrix. Cohorts A and B (badged V2) are the dormant audiences the current lifecycle program re-engages and what the Mailer Calendar and UK Non-Engagers hub target today. C to F cover the rest of the grid, engaged non-buyers, engaged buyers, quiet new-line buyers and multi-category near-VIPs, so every contact maps to one.

Engagement Levels

How a contact treats our messages, independent of whether they have bought. This band, crossed with purchase history, is what defines the Engagement Cohorts. It also sets send frequency: engaged contacts sustain more, disengaged ones get fewer, sharper touches to protect deliverability.

Product Cohorts

Grouped by what a customer buys: product category, finer collection, and purchase mode. This decides which world a message lives in and which cross-grade is natural. Products & Botanicals are one-time only, Coffee and Supplements lead with subscription.

Lifecycle Stages V1

The complete journey in 12 named cohorts across 5 stages. Each names a precise stage, defines exactly who lands in it, and states the audience and the trigger that should convert them.

RFM Segments V1

Recency, Frequency and Monetary scoring buckets from analytics. They drive send cadence in the 30-day Plan Calendar: higher-value engaged segments sustain more frequency, lapsed ones get fewer, sharper touches.

Behavioral Cohorts

Interest and behaviour groups the Smart Brain derives from purchase and browse patterns. They cut across lifecycle and RFM, and theme creative (which routine, which product world) rather than set cadence.

One customer can appear in several lenses at once. Always target by the most specific cohort a task needs. The names here are the source of truth, use them verbatim in every brief.
